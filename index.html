Look up a SKU

The SKU is the number on the shelf tag. Photo-scanning a barcode works in Chrome on Android; on iPhone, type it.

How to check if it's a penny
  1. Scan the item's own UPC barcode at self-checkout, not the yellow clearance tag. Scanning the tag can call an associate.
  2. If it rings up $0.01, pay. If the register asks for help, the store may pull the item. That's their call.
  3. A good sign before you scan: the item shows out of stock on homedepot.com for your store but is still on the shelf.
  4. Don't ask associates where penny items are. Home Depot treats penny items as ones that should have been pulled.

About this list

Where the items come from

Every item is a real Home Depot SKU that shoppers reported ringing up at $0.01, merged from public penny lists. Each item shows its sources, report count and last report date. Snapshot taken .

    What the labels mean
    • Receipt confirmed: someone posted a receipt or order showing $0.01.
    • Multi-source: two or more independent sources reported it.
    • Spreading: reports are coming in from more stores and states this week. These are the best bets for your store next.
    • Fading: last report is 2+ weeks old. Most stores have probably pulled it.
    The rules
    • Home Depot has no official penny list or penny policy. Penny prices are internal "pull this item" flags.
    • Penny items are store-specific. An item on this list may be full price, clearance or gone at your store.
    • A store can refuse the sale. A penny isn't yours until it's paid for.
    • Not affiliated with The Home Depot.
